ASTRONOMICAL REFERENCE GUIDE

Asteroid Types & Planetary Defense Science

1. What Are the Main Asteroid Spectral Types?

Using reflectance spectroscopy, astronomers analyze how sunlight reflects off an asteroid's surface to determine its chemical makeup:

  • C-Type (Carbonaceous): Accounts for ~75% of known asteroids. Dark as charcoal (albedo 0.03–0.08). Composed of primordial carbon compounds, hydrated clays, and organic molecules dating back to the formation of the solar nebula 4.6 billion years ago. Examples: Ceres, Bennu, Ryugu.
  • S-Type (Silicaceous / Stony): Accounts for ~17% of all asteroids and ~60% of observed Near-Earth Objects. Composed of nickel-iron metal mixed with iron- and magnesium-silicates (pyroxene and olivine). Moderate reflectivity (albedo 0.15–0.25). Examples: Eros, Itokawa.
  • M-Type (Metallic): Composed almost entirely of nickel-iron metal alloys. Likely remnants of the shattered cores of ancient proto-planets broken apart by giant collisions early in the solar system. Example: 16 Psyche.
  • V-Type (Vestoids): Basaltic crust fragments ejected from protoplanet 4 Vesta by colossal impacts billions of years ago.

2. The 4 Near-Earth Asteroid (NEA) Orbital Families

  • Apollo Asteroids: Earth-crossing orbits with semi-major axes larger than Earth's (> 1.0 AU). They cross Earth's path from the outside. Make up the majority of known NEAs.
  • Aten Asteroids: Earth-crossing orbits with semi-major axes smaller than Earth's (< 1.0 AU). Spend most of their orbits interior to Earth, crossing outward. Example: 99942 Apophis.
  • Amor Asteroids: Earth-approaching orbits between Earth and Mars (1.017 AU < perihelion < 1.3 AU). They do not currently cross Earth's orbit, but gravitational tugs can shift them over time.
  • Atira Asteroids (IEOs): Interior-Earth Objects whose orbits are completely within Earth's orbit. Extremely elusive because they are located toward the glare of the Sun.

3. What Defines a Potentially Hazardous Asteroid (PHA)?

NASA and the International Astronomical Union classify an asteroid as a Potentially Hazardous Asteroid (PHA) if it satisfies two rigorous criteria:

  1. Minimum Orbit Intersection Distance (MOID): The closest point between the asteroid's orbit and Earth's orbit must be 0.05 AU or less (~7.48 million km / 19.5 Lunar Distances).
  2. Size / Brightness Threshold: Absolute magnitude H ≤ 22.0, corresponding to an estimated diameter of 140 meters (460 feet) or larger.

Important: PHA classification does not mean an asteroid is on an impact course. It means the object is large enough and approaches closely enough that its orbit must be catalogued and monitored with high precision over decades to centuries.

4. Real Planetary Defense: How NASA Defends Earth

Planetary defense relies on genuine physics and early astronomical detection rather than science-fiction shields:

  • Kinetic Impactor (DART Mission): In September 2022, NASA's Double Asteroid Redirection Test (DART) spacecraft successfully impacted asteroid Dimorphos at 22,500 km/h, altering its orbital period by 32 minutes—proving kinetic deflection works in real space.
  • Gravity Tractor: A heavy spacecraft flying alongside a threatening asteroid for months to years uses its tiny mutual gravitational pull to slowly tow the asteroid off an impact trajectory.
  • Early Warning Surveys: Ground observatories (Pan-STARRS, ATLAS, Catalina Sky Survey) and upcoming space telescopes (NEO Surveyor) continuously map space to find objects decades before potential close encounters.
